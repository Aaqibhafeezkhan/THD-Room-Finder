THD Room Finder: Redesign & Rescope

Turning a competent room database into the fastest answer on campus to one question — where can I sit, right now, for as long as I need?

00  The short version

One job, done in a glance

Room Finder already does something genuinely useful: it tells a student which of THD's ~289 classrooms are free, using only public THabella data, with no account and no backend. That's the right product. But today it looks and behaves like a database browser — an alphabetical list keyed by room code, a binary free/occupied banner. It answers "which rooms are empty?" when the question a student actually has is more specific: "which room is free, and for how long?"

This project keeps the scope tight and the THD identity intact — same THD blue, same Roboto, same Material foundation — then sharpens two things:

01

Duration is the headline

Every room answers "free for how long" — free 2h 10m — not just a binary empty/occupied. Students need to know if they can finish, not just start.

02

From database browser to a clear answer

The home count flows into a confident, glanceable list ranked by how long each room stays free and filtered by building — not an alphabetical room-code dump that leaves the work to you.

We considered ranking rooms by proximity, but it doesn't earn its place here. THD's campus is compact — roughly eight minutes' walk from the main campus to the furthest building in town — so where a free room is matters far less than how long it's free. Distance is out; duration is in.

01  Where it is today

An honest snapshot

The current product is a native Android/iOS app plus a static PWA, recreated here faithfully. The flow is three screens: a home screen with a large count of rooms free now; a list of free rooms with building-filter chips and a datetime lookup; and a room detail with a free/occupied banner and the day's schedule.

Visually it is textbook Material 3 — Roboto, THD blue #1565C0, teal "free" #00796B / red "occupied" #B3261E, soft elevated cards, a 64px app bar. It is clean, competent, and unmistakably THD. The problem isn't the look — it's the hierarchy: the screen leads with a count and a code, and buries the one fact a student is really after.

It answers correctly. It just answers the question one step removed from the one you're really asking.— The design problem in one line

02  Design critique

What to keep, what's holding it back

Keep / protect

  • The radical simplicity — no accounts, no backend, no config.
  • The big-number home screen. Glanceable and confident.
  • THD blue, Roboto, and the Material foundation — the identity is fine; keep it.
  • Green = free / red = occupied. Learned, load-bearing, never touch.
  • Honest data: real room codes, real German building names.

Holding it back

  • Lists sort by room code, not by what you need — how long it's free.
  • "Free / occupied" is binary; hides the crucial duration.
  • No sense of start vs finish — can I settle in, or just pass through?
  • The home count is a dead-end; the list below feels disconnected from it.
  • Type hierarchy is flat — everything shouts, so nothing leads.

03  Who it's for

Three students, one app

The redesign is anchored to concrete moments, not personas-in-the-abstract. Every screen should serve at least one of these.

The gap-filler · between classes

"I've got 50 minutes free. Which empty room can I actually settle into for the whole time, not get kicked out of in ten?"

The planner · the night before

"Our group needs a room Thursday at 14:00 for two hours. What'll be free, and big enough for six?"

The regular · same spot, every day

"Is my usual quiet room on the second floor of J open right now?"

04  Design thesis

Answer-first, not browse-first

The mental model is a transit departure board crossed with a calm weather app: authoritative, instantly scannable, and trustworthy at a glance. You don't browse a departure board — you find your answer in under a second and move. Room Finder should feel the same — rendered entirely in THD's own Material system, just with a sharper spine.

Concretely, that means an information hierarchy that always leads with how long a room is free, status you can read peripherally, and a count that flows directly into a ranked, scannable list. The product stays resolutely flat and data-driven — no stock photos of students laughing at laptops, no re-skin away from THD blue — but every screen earns its glance.

Open, glance, go. If a student needs more than one tap to know whether a room fits their hour, we've failed.— Success, defined

05  Visual & interaction direction

What the redesign looks like

Lead with the answer

The home count stays — Roboto's light display weight, THD blue — but it no longer dead-ends. It flows straight into a list of free rooms, each row leading with how long it's free and its seat count, ranked longest-free first. Room code and building stay legible but step back in the hierarchy.

Palette — THD's own, formalized

No new brand colour. We keep THD blue as the accent and the learned green/red status, and simply tokenize them so they're applied consistently — including a third state for "free, but closing soon." Same Material surfaces, just used with more intent.

Motion & surface

Stay flat and fast — short 120–160ms transitions, no glassmorphism, no gradients, Material elevation kept light. The one new texture is a subtle "board" rhythm in how rows tick over when data refreshes. Full dark theme, as today.

06  Feature scope

What's in, what's later, what's out

Deliberately conservative. The product's value is its focus; new features earn their place or wait.

In scope — this redesign core
Free-for-duration
Each room shows how long it stays free — and flags "closing soon" — not just a binary state.
Duration-ranked list
Free rooms ordered by how long they stay open, longest-free first; the count flows straight into it.
Building filter
Fast chips, now the primary way to narrow — the natural axis once distance is off the table.
Time-travel lookup
Pick any future date/time to check ahead — kept and made prominent.
Room detail + schedule
Status banner, seats/facilities/contact, the day's timeline.
Full dark theme + PWA

Saved rooms

Capacity & equipment filters
"Seats 6+", "has projector" for the group-work job.

Out of scope — for now won't

Accounts / login

Not the job. Adds weight, removes focus.

07  What the design system must contain

From "Material clone" to an owned, documented system

Once the direction is approved, the design system gets formalized around the thesis — keeping THD blue, Roboto, and Material roles, but encoding the duration-first hierarchy as real tokens and components:

  • Tokens — the existing THD blue palette and Roboto type scale, formalized; light Material elevation; a 4px grid retained.
  • Status as a first-class token set — free / occupied / closing-soon, with the free-for-duration formatting baked in.
  • Components — Home count, Room row (status + duration), Status banner, Time picker, Filter chips, App bar, Map pin (Phase 2).
  • UI kits — the redesigned mobile flow and a refreshed marketing page, both as live, tweakable templates.
